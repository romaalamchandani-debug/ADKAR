Change Management Framework

What is the ADKAR Model?

ADKAR is a change-management framework from Prosci that explains the five outcomes an individual needs to move through for a change to be adopted and sustained: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement.

ADKAR at a glance

A

Awareness

Understand why the change is necessary and what problem it addresses.

Key question: “Why are we changing?”
D

Desire

Choose to support the change and participate in making it happen.

Key question: “What’s in it for me?”
K

Knowledge

Know what to do differently, including new processes, skills, or behaviors.

Key question: “How do I change?”
A

Ability

Demonstrate the new behavior in real work, not just understand it conceptually.

Key question: “Can I do it effectively?”
R

Reinforcement

Use recognition, measurement, feedback, and follow-through to keep the change in place.

Key question: “How do we make it stick?”

The ADKAR journey

Why ADKAR is useful

  • It focuses on the individual experience of change rather than only the project plan.
  • It helps identify where adoption is getting stuck.
  • It separates different barriers: a person may understand the reason for change but still lack the skill or confidence to perform the new behavior.
  • It gives change leaders a simple structure for communication, training, coaching, and reinforcement.
Example

Introducing a new LMS

Awareness: Employees understand why the company is replacing the old LMS.

Desire: They see the benefits and are willing to use it.

Knowledge: They learn how to log in, enroll, complete courses, and find records.

Ability: They successfully use the LMS during real work.

Reinforcement: Managers recognize usage, completion is tracked, and support continues after launch.

What to do when people get stuck

SignalLikely ADKAR gapUseful intervention
“Why are we doing this?”AwarenessExplain business drivers, consequences of not changing, and expected outcomes.
“I don't want to do this.”DesireAddress WIIFM, concerns, leadership commitment, and personal impact.
“I don't know how.”KnowledgeProvide training, demonstrations, guides, examples, and practice.
“I know how, but I can't do it yet.”AbilityUse coaching, hands-on practice, job aids, feedback, and time to build proficiency.
“We went back to the old way.”ReinforcementUse recognition, metrics, accountability, follow-up, and visible leadership support.
Trainer takeaway: Training primarily strengthens the Knowledge and Ability parts of ADKAR. Sustainable adoption also requires the organization to build Awareness and Desire before training, and Reinforcement after the change is launched.
